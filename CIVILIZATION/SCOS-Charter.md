# SCOS Charter

## Purpose

The SCOS Charter establishes the foundational principles from which the MIN Civilization system emerges.

It exists to define the civilizational assumptions that precede governance, institutions, and operational systems.

---

## Mission

Create a stable foundation for civilization.

Preserve meaningful knowledge.

Enable long-term continuity.

Support sustainable evolution across generations.

---

## Core Statement

A civilization should be capable of preserving knowledge, maintaining continuity, and evolving beyond the limitations of any individual participant.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Continuity

Civilization should survive participant turnover, leadership change, and structural evolution.

---

### Preservation

Knowledge, history, and reasoning should be preserved whenever possible.

---

### Traceability

Important concepts should maintain identifiable origins and historical lineage.

---

### Governance

Rules should be documented and understandable.

Authority should not depend solely on individuals.

---

### Evolution

Civilization should be capable of adaptation while maintaining continuity.

---

### Stewardship

Participants are temporary stewards.

Civilization is intended to outlast its contributors.

## Objectives

1. Preserve continuity.
2. Preserve knowledge.
3. Preserve history.
4. Enable future participation.
5. Support long-term evolution.

---

## Success Criteria

The charter succeeds when:

- Civilization remains understandable.
- Knowledge remains discoverable.
- Governance remains functional.
- Concepts remain traceable.
- Future participants can continue development.

---

## Relationship

The SCOS Charter serves as the philosophical and constitutional origin of the civilization framework.

It precedes and guides all subordinate charters, governance systems, registries, and preservation mechanisms.
